**Format:** Standard 2026 (Regulation Marks H / I / J)
**Archetype cost:** roughly $18–$28 in singles
**Difficulty:** Beginner — a fast, aggressive deck with a simple plan

Fighting is the "hit fast" deck. Instead of building up over many turns, you get an attacker onto the field early, attach Energy, and start taking Prize cards right away. It's a great first deck because it teaches you the most important pressure in the game: race your opponent, and make them react to *you*.

This is one half of the **Fire vs. Fighting Battle Box** — two decks built to be played against each other so two new players can learn the game together. It also stands completely on its own.

> **New to the game?** This page is written for a first-time player. Fighting is the more aggressive of the two Battle Box decks — if you like going first and applying pressure, start here.

## What This Deck Teaches You

Instead of "matchups," here's what you'll actually learn by playing Fighting — the fundamentals that carry over to every aggressive deck:

- **Attacking early.** You don't wait to set up a big Stage 2. You get a Basic attacker like **Hawlucha** going and start dealing damage on turn one or two.
- **Energy attachment as tempo.** With only one Energy attachment per turn, deciding *which* attacker gets it — and when — is the whole game. Fighting teaches you to make that call under pressure.
- **Prize racing.** The player who takes all their Prize cards first wins. This deck teaches you to count Prizes, apply pressure, and force your opponent to react instead of building their own plan.

## Your First Few Turns

A forgiving, plain-language game plan. You don't have to do this perfectly — just apply pressure.

1. **Lead with a Basic attacker.** Get **Hawlucha** into the Active spot (or on the Bench, ready to move up). Use **Buddy-Buddy Poffin** or **Poké Ball** to find your Pokémon.
2. **Attach a Fighting Energy right away.** The sooner you can attack, the sooner you're taking Prizes.
3. **Attack as soon as you can.** Even small early damage matters — you're setting up knockouts a turn or two ahead.
4. **Use your draw cards to keep the pressure on.** **Naveen**, **Judge**, and **Poké Pad** refill your hand so you always have a next attacker ready.
5. **Pick your target.** Use **Boss's Orders** or **Pokémon Catcher** to drag up the Pokémon you can knock out for a Prize — don't let the opponent hide their weak Pokémon on the Bench.
6. **Keep swinging.** If an attacker falls, **Night Stretcher** and **Tarragon** bring your Pokémon and Fighting Energy back so the pressure never stops.

## How to Play It

Fighting wins by never giving the opponent a quiet turn. Go first if you can, get an attacker online immediately, and start trading — you want the game to be a race, because a race favors the faster deck. Against a slower "build up" deck like Delphox, your job is to take Prizes before they get fully set up. Use **Boss's Orders** to reach past their Active Pokémon and knock out the fragile Pokémon they're trying to grow on the Bench.
