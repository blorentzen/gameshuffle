Cynthia's Garchomp ex survived the April 2026 rotation as one of the format's best "fair" attackers: a 330 HP Stage 2 that draws cards while it attacks and hits for 260+ on a two-Energy cost. If you want a resilient, high-damage deck that punishes the format's disruption and spread decks rather than folding to them, this is one of the strongest picks in your price range.

## Is Cynthia's Garchomp still good after the 2026 rotation?

Short answer: yes. Rotation cost the deck some old Trainer support, but Garchomp's core line — Gible, Gabite, Garchomp ex, and the Cynthia's Roserade damage-booster — all live in _Destined Rivals_ and were completely untouched. The deck lost nothing from its identity; it only had to rebuild its draw engine around the current legal Supporters. Post-rotation tournament results have kept it in the competitive conversation, with a positive matchup into the format's most-played deck (Dragapult) and a clear, learnable game plan.

The one thing to know before you build: **watch for Grass decks.** Garchomp is weak to Grass, so Mega Meganium ex and Crustle are its worst matchups. Everything else is playable.

## What You Need to Buy vs. What Swaps

Not every slot is locked. Some cards define the deck and have no real substitute; others are flex slots you can fill from cards you likely already own or can pull from GameShuffle stock. Here's the honest breakdown.

### 🔒 Locked — core identity, don't substitute

These are the deck. Cutting them changes what you're playing.

- **4 Cynthia's Gible / 4 Cynthia's Gabite / 3 Cynthia's Garchomp ex** — the attacker line. Gabite at 4 is non-negotiable: its Ability tutors the entire Cynthia line out of your deck, which is the deck's consistency engine (it plays the same role Drakloak does in Dragapult).
- **4 Cynthia's Roselia / 3 Cynthia's Roserade** — Roserade's +30 damage is what pushes Garchomp's numbers into one-shot range.
- **3 Cynthia's Power Weight** — the +70 HP tool that lets Garchomp reach 400 HP and become genuinely hard to knock out.
- **4 Lillie's Determination** — the format's premier early-game draw Supporter now that Professor's Research and Iono are gone. This is the single most important card to acquire.

### 🔁 Swappable — reasonable budget alternatives

If you're building on a budget or from stock on hand, these slots flex:

| Optimal card | Budget swap | What you give up |
|--------------|-------------|------------------|
| 1 Neo Upper Energy (~$19) | 1 extra Basic Fighting Energy | A little recovery-turn acceleration; the deck still functions. **This is the first cut for a budget build.** |
| 2 Hilda (~$2+ ea) | 1 Judge + 1 Poké Pad | Some raw card advantage; you lean harder on Items for draw |
| 2 Premium Power Pro | 1 Cynthia's Power Weight + 1 Fighting Gong | Burst damage ceiling; you keep bulk and search instead |
| 2 Pokégear 3.0 | 2 Poké Pad | Supporter-finding reliability; still get card flow |
| 1 Rare Candy | 1 extra Cynthia's Gabite | Nothing major — Gabite's Ability already fetches your evolutions, so Rare Candy is close to optional here |

**Budget floor:** dropping Neo Upper Energy, one Hilda, and softening the Pokégear count brings the deck close to **$45–$55** while keeping every match-defining card intact.

## How to Play It

**Go second if you can.** The extra Energy attachment and the draw from Corkscrew Dive make the second-player game plan smoother.

**Turn one, prioritize:** two Cynthia's Gible, one to two Cynthia's Roselia, and Shaymin on the bench. Shaymin's Flower Curtain protects your non-Rule-Box Pokémon (your Gibles, Gabites, and Roserade line) from spread and snipe attacks while they're still fragile.

**The engine:** Gabite's Ability searches out your Garchomp, more Gabite, Roserade, and Spiritomb — this is how the deck sets up without relying on luck. Lean on it every turn.

**The attack math:**

- Corkscrew Dive: 100 damage and draw until you have 6 cards. This is your setup-turn attack — you're chipping while refilling your hand and it protects you from hand disruption.
- Draconic Buster: 260 for two Fighting Energy (you discard them afterward).
- With one Cynthia's Roserade in play: 290.
- With two Roserade: 320.
- Add two Premium Power Pro in a turn: up to **350+** — enough to one-shot almost anything in the format.

**Cynthia's Spiritomb** is your tech attacker: its Raging Curse scales with damage counters, so it punishes spread decks that put counters on your board.

| Mega Meganium ex | ❌ Hard | Grass weakness — doubles your damage taken |
| Crustle | ❌ Hard | Grass weakness, plus damage-prevention tech |
| Spread / disruption decks | ✅ Favorable | Corkscrew Dive draws through disruption; Spiritomb punishes counters |
| Most ex-based aggro | ➖ Even to favorable | 400 HP Garchomp is hard to two-shot; you race back with 260+ |

**Tech tip:** Rocky Fighting Energy (POR 87) protects the equipped Pokémon from attack effects — it shuts off Paralysis, Sleep, and Confusion on your Garchomp, so you only have to worry about raw damage.

## Playing It as a Cynthia Theme Deck

This list doubles as a **Cynthia tribe deck** — that's why it carries both the Competitive and Themed badges. Almost every named card is a _Cynthia's_ card: the entire Gible → Gabite → Garchomp ex attacker line, the Roselia → Roserade damage engine, Cynthia's Spiritomb, Cynthia's Power Weight, and the Team Rocket's Watchtower stadium all share the Cynthia identity from _Destined Rivals_.

If you want to lean **all the way** into the theme, the only "neutral" slots are a handful of format-standard draw and search Supporters (Lillie's Determination, Judge, Buddy-Buddy Poffin). You can swap some of those for additional Cynthia-flavored support at a small consistency cost — but the beauty here is you don't have to choose between theme and power: the competitive build already *is* the Cynthia tribe. It's the rare deck that's fully on-theme and genuinely tournament-viable at the same time.
