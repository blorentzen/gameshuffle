**Format:** Standard 2026 (Regulation Marks H / I / J)
**Archetype cost:** roughly $35–$55 in singles
**Difficulty:** Intermediate — the combo has moving parts, but the payoff is worth it

This is the deck that makes opponents groan. Instead of attacking for big numbers, it **places damage counters all over the board and then teleports them onto whatever it wants to knock out** — then gusts up your target and finishes it off. It's a genuine, playable gimmick built from the _Destined Rivals_ Team Rocket cards, and it is *deeply* annoying to sit across from.

There's no win-rate claim here — it's a meme deck. But the combo is real, and when it goes off it feels incredible.

## The Combo

This is what you came for. Here's the whole gimmick, step by step:

1. **Seed damage counters as you evolve.** Evolving up the **Zubat → Golbat → Team Rocket's Crobat ex** line places damage counters onto your opponent's Pokémon on the way up — that's your free chip damage, no attack required.
2. **Set up Orbeetle.** Get **Team Rocket's Orbeetle** into play (via the Blipbug line, or skip Dottler with **Rare Candy**). Its **Rocket Brain** Ability lets you *move damage counters around among your own Pokémon* — this is how you gather counters onto one Wobbuffet.
3. **Load up Wobbuffet.** Funnel counters onto **Team Rocket's Wobbuffet**, then use its **Rocket Mirror** effect to **dump all those counters onto the opponent's Active Pokémon** in one shot.
4. **Gust the target.** Use **Team Rocket's Giovanni** to drag up whichever opponent Pokémon you want to finish — usually one you've already been chipping.
5. **Finish with Persian ex.** Clean up the loaded, gusted target with **Team Rocket's Persian ex** and take your Prizes.

The whole point: you decide where the damage goes, so the opponent can never keep their key Pokémon safe on the Bench.

## Why It's Fun (and Annoying)

Spread decks win by taking control of the board instead of the biggest attacker. It's annoying to play against because **nothing your opponent does is safe** — benching a fragile Pokémon to set it up just hands you another target to load counters onto. It rewards planning two turns ahead, and pulling off the full teleport-and-gust sequence is one of the most satisfying combos in the format.

> **Prize note — you're running `ex` Pokémon.** Both **Crobat ex** and **Persian ex** give up **two Prize cards** when knocked out. Since this deck wants them active, respect the risk — don't leave a fragile `ex` in front if you're about to lose the exchange.
