**Format:** Standard 2026 (Regulation Marks H / I / J)
**Archetype cost:** roughly $20–$35 in singles
**Difficulty:** Intermediate — simple pieces, but you have to win the prize-trade math

Everybody plays two-prize `ex` decks. This one doesn't — and that's the whole point. It's a **mono-Fire swarm of single-prize attackers** that only ever gives up **one** Prize card at a time. Against a deck full of `ex` Pokémon, every trade is in your favor: they take one prize for your little guy, you take one for their two-prize monster. Do that a few times and the math wins the game.

It's a budget, all-commons-and-rares underdog deck — no `ex`, no chase cards, no win-rate claim. Just good prize math and a lot of small fires.

## Why the Prize Math Wins

Here's the hook, in plain terms:

1. **Everything you play is single-prize.** Every attacker on this list is a non-`ex` Pokémon — when one gets knocked out, your opponent takes **one** Prize card.
2. **They're mostly two-prize.** The format is full of `ex` Pokémon that give up **two** Prizes each.
3. **So every trade favors you.** If you knock out one of their `ex` and they knock out one of your little guys, the prize race is 1-for-2 in your favor. Keep the swarm coming — you have far more bodies than they have attackers — and you'll out-trade them to six prizes first.

**Ethan's Typhlosion** fuels the board with Fire, **N's Darmanitan** and **Salazzle** hit hard for their cost, and **Boss's Orders** lets you target the `ex` they're trying to power up on the bench before it comes online.

## Why It's Fun

There's real satisfaction in beating the "good" decks with a pile of commons. This is the deck that punishes the metagame's reliance on big two-prize threats — an honest, grindy underdog that rewards knowing the prize math better than your opponent does.
